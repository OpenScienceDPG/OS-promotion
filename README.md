# DPG Open Science catalog

This repository maintains an auditable catalog of substantive Open Science practices in publications involving DPG authors. PDFs are inputs; the durable knowledge lives in small YAML records and an append-only screening ledger. A single-page Quarto showcase, styled after the University of Padova web identity, is generated from those records and published from `docs/` with GitHub Pages.

The current workflow keeps the useful original sequence:

1. local deterministic PDF screening;
2. semantic review of promising or ambiguous cases;
3. durable records, evidence, and an audit event;
4. validation and generation of public-safe site data.

PDF text is extracted locally with `pdftools`. PDFs must never be uploaded to public GROBID or other document-processing services without explicit authorization.

## Repository map

- `inbox/`: new private PDF inputs, held only until finalization deletes them. PDFs are never stored in the repository (`*.pdf` is git-ignored).
- `records/<id>/record.yml`: canonical publication records, including lifecycle state and evidence.
- `ledger/screening.csv`: canonical history of screening/finalization events, including rejects.
- `reports/`: transient CSVs for the current run; safe to regenerate.
- `selected/`: legacy migration notes and manifests (no PDFs).
- `index.qmd`, `_quarto.yml`, and `styles.scss`: Quarto source in the repository root.
- `assets/`: public images and badges used by the site.
- `config/badges.yml`: the controlled Open Science practices and their badge label, image, and description link.
- `data/`: generated public-safe site data. PDFs and evidence passages are never copied here.
- `docs/`: rendered public site for GitHub Pages; generated by `quarto render`, committed, never edited by hand.

Canonical files are `records/**/record.yml` and `ledger/screening.csv`. The files in `reports/`, `data/`, and `docs/` are generated and must not be edited manually. `selected/manifest.csv`, `selected/initial_selection.csv`, and `selected/SELECTION_NOTES.md` are retained as migration history, not as the current source of truth.

The record format and lifecycle states are documented in [`records/README.md`](records/README.md).

## Ordinary use

Put new PDFs anywhere below `inbox/`, then ask an agent to **process inbox**. The agent follows `AGENTS.md`: it runs the deterministic screen, reviews only cases that require meaning-based judgment, records concise observable evidence, finalizes safely, validates the repository, and regenerates site data.

After finalization every PDF (kept, rejected or duplicate) is deleted from `inbox/`; keep originals outside the repository. Known hashes are recognized, and an existing human override is never replaced by automation.

Alternatively, ask **“proseguire il lavoro”**: the agent uses the latest dated
`inbox/list-YYYY-MM-DD.xlsx` as the publication inventory, filters to 2025 onward,
compares against accepted and rejected records, and searches for **5–7 new papers**,
taken in priority order (2026 first) from the keyword shortlist built by
`scripts/00-shortlist.R` (`reports/candidati-keyword-YYYY-MM-DD.csv`).
Problems needing a human decision are logged in `notes/open-issues.md`.
It may use the connected browser and existing university access when available.
The workbook remains intact; it supplies bibliography and Unpaywall OA type,
not evidence of other Open Science practices. OA badges accept gold, bronze or
hybrid; OA never determines inclusion. Both workflows preserve decisions in
records and the ledger; unavailable papers remain unresolved rather than rejected.
See `AGENTS.md` for deduplication, access-attempt tracking and the batch workflow.

## Review modes

**Ordinary mode (default)** accepts a paper's explicit declaration that a
specific resource (data, code, materials, protocol, preregistration or another
qualifying practice) is openly available without access conditions, only if
the paper also supplies an actual supporting resource link. Generic statements,
bare links and availability on request do not qualify. Published view-only
links qualify when presented as providing access without login or authorization.
The agent reads the declaration and link in the paper but does not open or test
the resource. Qualifying practices receive their badges and normal inclusion.

Records use `screening.review_mode: ordinary`, evidence
`verification: unverified` and `curation.resource_review.status: pending`,
assigned to Enrico. Pending resource checks do not block `ready`.

**In-depth mode ("modalità approfondita")**, explicitly requested for a unit
of work, adds an independent check that the linked resources exist, correspond
to the paper, support the claimed practice and are accessible without login,
requests or further authorization. Records use `screening.review_mode: in_depth`
and dated evidence of the actual checks.

Identity/scope, deduplication, scientific-summary review and validation apply
in both modes. Open Access is recorded separately and is neither necessary nor
sufficient for inclusion. Historical `review_mode: rapid` records retain their
original provenance. See [the agent protocol](AGENTS.md) and
[record conventions](records/README.md).

## Manual commands

Requires R plus `pdftools` and `yaml`.

```powershell
Rscript scripts/01-screen.R
# Resolve every "review" row in reports/decisions.csv.
Rscript scripts/02-finalize.R
Rscript scripts/04-validate.R
Rscript scripts/05-build-site-data.R
quarto render        # writes docs/
Rscript scripts/04-validate.R
```

For a kept paper, `reports/decisions.csv` must name at least one controlled practice and provide a concise evidence note, normally with a page/location and direct resource URL. Low-scoring algorithmic rejects may remain rejects unless their extracted evidence suggests a missed substantive practice.

## Audit and human control

To understand a decision, start in `ledger/screening.csv`, then open the corresponding `record.yml`. Positive claims link to concise PDF/repository evidence; verbose agent reasoning is intentionally not stored.

Human corrections are authoritative. Set the record's `human_override` block (decision, person, date, and note) and append a `human-override` event to the ledger. Validation checks the block, and later automated runs preserve it. Changed evidence may trigger re-review but cannot silently erase the override.

## Public site

`scripts/05-build-site-data.R` derives `data/publications.csv` and `data/listing.md` (filter chips plus one HTML card per publication) from eligible canonical records. Each card links to a modal with the record's `curation.public_summary`: an agent-drafted, plain-language Italian paragraph (about abstract length) that can be linked directly as `#sintesi-<record id>`. The generated data include only public-facing bibliography, labels, explanations, and resource links—never PDF paths, hashes, or extracted passages.

The showcase shows only publications from 2025 on (`site_min_year`); earlier eligible records remain in `records/` but are hidden. `quarto render` produces a single showcase page in `docs/`. To publish, enable GitHub Pages with *Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → `main` / `docs`*. Colors and type follow the unipd.it design system (Rosso Unipd `#9B0014`, Inter, Noto Serif italic titles); they are defined at the top of `styles.scss`.

Everything about a badge is set in one place, [`config/badges.yml`](config/badges.yml): one entry per controlled practice with its `label`, its image file in `assets/badges/` (`badge`), the plain-language Italian text of its pop-up (`description`), and the `link` to the page describing it with its `link_text`. To change a text or link, or swap an image, edit that file (drop a new image in `assets/badges/` first), then rebuild the site; `scripts/04-validate.R` checks that every entry is complete and its image exists. `open-data.png`, `open-materials.png`, and `preregistered.png` are the official Center for Open Science badges (CC BY 4.0, <https://osf.io/tvyxz/>); the `.svg` files are drafts in the same style for practices without an official COS badge.

Clicking a badge on a card (or in its summary pop-up) opens a small pop-up with the badge shown larger, its `description`, and below it the `link`. The COS badges point to the OSF wiki [“1. View the Badges”](https://osf.io/tvyxz/wiki/1.%20View%20the%20Badges/), Registered Report to the [COS Registered Reports page](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports), and the draft badges to the OSF badges project. The filter chips only filter; they do not open the pop-up.

Semantic verification, DPG-author confirmation, plain-language summaries, and the choice to publish or feature an item remain agent-assisted or human-supervised curation tasks.

Migration starts from the ten accepted publications for which files and notes still exist. The other 195 papers mentioned in the historical first screening cannot be reconstructed publication-by-publication because the previous workflow retained no durable reject identifiers or decisions. Every future finalized item, including rejects, receives both a record and a ledger event.
